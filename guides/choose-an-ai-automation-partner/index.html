How to choose an AI automation partner

Most small businesses only buy AI help once or twice, so it's hard to tell a good partner from a good pitch. These are the questions that separate them.

Request a free discovery session

Published 2026-10-31 by VisionzLab

Start with one workflow, not a strategy

The best first project is one piece of work your team does every week: reading supplier quotes, keying orders, sorting a shared inbox, chasing renewals. It has inputs you can show a partner, a result you can check, and a person who knows when the output is wrong.

A partner who wants to start with a company-wide AI strategy before touching any real work is asking you to pay for a document. Ask them to pick the workflow with you instead.

Eight questions to ask

  • Will you show it working on our own documents before we commit to a full build?
  • Who approves what the AI produces, and where does that step sit in the workflow?
  • Which accounts and permissions does it need, and can we turn them off ourselves?
  • Who owns the prompts, code, configuration and accounts when the project ends?
  • What does it cost to run each month after it's built: software, AI usage and support?
  • How will we measure whether it worked, and who measures it?
  • What happens when it meets something it hasn't seen before?
  • If we stop working together, how do we keep it running or switch it off cleanly?

Red flags

  • A demo on the vendor's sample data, but no offer to test on yours.
  • Accuracy or savings figures quoted before anyone has seen your work.
  • No named person on your side who approves the AI's output.
  • Accounts, keys or code held by the vendor with no handover plan.
  • A large up-front build with no smaller first step you can judge.

Make the first step small enough to judge

Ask for a short, paid first step with a defined deliverable: a prototype on a sample of your real inputs, a measure of success you agree up front, and a costed plan for what comes next. If it doesn't work, you've spent a little and learned a lot. If it does, you have evidence for the bigger decision.

That is how our own Discovery Sprint works, and it's a fair test to apply to any partner, including us.

Questions

Which AI automation company is best for a small business?

The one that will test on your own work before you commit, names who approves the output, hands over ownership of what it builds, and can tell you the monthly running cost. Use the eight questions above to compare any shortlist.

How much should an AI automation project cost?

It depends on the workflow and how much help you want, from training your team to having a partner build and run it. Ask for a fixed price for a defined first step, and for the monthly running cost, before you sign anything larger.

Do we need a technical person on staff?

Not to start. You need someone who knows the work well enough to judge the output. A good partner sets up the technical side and leaves you a playbook, or runs it for you.

Should we just use ChatGPT or Copilot ourselves?

Often, yes, for individual tasks. A partner earns their fee when the work runs across systems, needs approval steps, or has to run reliably every day without someone prompting it.
