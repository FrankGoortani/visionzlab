AI agent, automation tool or a new hire?

Three different tools for three different kinds of work. Picking the wrong one is the most common reason automation projects disappoint.

Request a free discovery session

Published 2026-10-31 by VisionzLab

Rules-based automation: fixed inputs, fixed rules

If the information arrives in the same shape every time and the steps never change, a rules-based tool is cheaper and more predictable than AI. Moving form submissions into a spreadsheet, sending a reminder three days before a due date, or copying a new customer into your CRM are rules-based jobs. Tools such as Zapier, Make, Power Automate or your own software's built-in rules do this well.

An AI agent: messy inputs, judgement within limits

If the work starts with reading something written for people (emails, PDFs, scanned forms, supplier quotes) and needs judgement inside clear limits, that is where an AI agent helps. It can read the request, match it to your catalogue or records, draft the reply or the entry, and flag what it isn't sure about.

The safe pattern is that the agent prepares and a person approves. Your team stops re-keying and starts checking, which is faster and keeps the decisions with them.

A person: relationships, accountability, rare cases

Negotiating with a key customer, deciding on an exception that sets a precedent, or owning the result when something goes wrong is work for a person. So is any task that happens a few times a year: it isn't worth automating.

Signs a workflow is ready for an agent

  • It happens every week, often enough that the time adds up.
  • Someone re-keys or copies information from documents or email into a system.
  • You can show twenty real examples of the input and the correct result.
  • A person on the team can tell quickly whether the output is right.
  • The systems involved have an API, an import, or a usable screen workflow.

Most real workflows use all three

A typical result: rules move the files, an agent reads them and drafts the entry, and a person approves the exceptions. Deciding which step belongs to which is most of the design work.

Questions

Is an AI agent cheaper than hiring?

Sometimes, for the reading and re-keying part of a role, but not for the judgement and relationships. Compare the agent's build and monthly running cost with the hours it takes back, measured on your own work. Our hours-back calculator is a quick first estimate.

What is the difference between an AI agent and automation?

Automation follows fixed rules on structured data. An AI agent can read unstructured input such as email and documents, and decide within limits you set. Many workflows combine both.

Can an AI agent make mistakes?

Yes. That's why the agent should flag uncertain cases and a person should approve anything that matters before it is sent or saved.
